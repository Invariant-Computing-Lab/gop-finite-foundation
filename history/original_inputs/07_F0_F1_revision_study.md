# A limited F1 revision and a fresh mechanism obstruction

This prospective auxiliary study concerns the declared resolved classical generator. It does not use the ten-family locked prediction data and does not establish a theorem about all possible hidden realizations.

F0-G permits drift polynomials of degree at most two. In the scalar physical coordinate x, the confining system dx/dt=-x-x^3 is an overdamped gradient flow of V=x^2/2+x^4/4, with unit mobility in nondimensional units. Thus it is not an arbitrary lookup table introduced only to beat the candidate. On [-1,1], x^3=(T3(x)+3x)/4. By the four alternating extrema of T3, the best uniform quadratic approximation has irreducible error 1/4. To see the lower bound directly, a degree<=2 improvement strictly below 1/4 would have to alternate in sign at four ordered extrema; its difference from the candidate minimax polynomial would then have at least three roots, impossible. Therefore the frozen 0.01 instantaneous-drift requirement fails.

F1-G changes only the maximum classical drift degree from two to three. It adds one coefficient in the scalar instance (and, for d full state coordinates, up to d*choose(d+2,3) cubic coefficients before locality constraints). Sensor maps, channel capacity, quantum library, coordinate rules and all other assumptions remain fixed. This repairs the cubic class obstruction exactly by allowing c1=-1,c3=-1. It does not repair other physical obligations.

The new prospective test mechanism is dx/dt=-x-x^5, another confining gradient flow, V=x^2/2+x^6/6. It is not used for F1 estimation. Since x^5=(T5+5T3+10T1)/16, the best uniform degree<=3 drift is -11x/16-5x^3/4 and its sharp error is 1/16. Six alternating extrema imply that a strictly better degree<=3 polynomial would require at least five zeros, impossible. This also excludes degree-four improvement; it certainly excludes degree three. The observed grid residual checks the formula but is not the proof.

Training uses 256 randomly assigned, measured initial positions and their reference endpoints at h=1e-4 from a separate DOP853 solver. The measured finite-difference drift includes known additive synthetic noise. Fitted F0/F1 polynomial coefficients and RK4 trajectories are saved. Finite differences have O(h) bias: a nonzero F1 fitted error on the cubic does not negate its exact representability. The tests report class bounds separately from fitted errors.

These analytic counterexamples were selected during construction. They are openly declared falsification witnesses, not a blind discovery. The fresh mechanism is a prospective transfer test and a class proof; it is not a new ten-family confirmatory replication. No further revision is tuned to make the result positive. A future F2 must justify changes to the restriction itself, rather than continually increasing polynomial degree and calling that a new physical explanation.
